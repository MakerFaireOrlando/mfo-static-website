description-long: "Most people assume recovering metal from e-waste or ore takes a smelter. This exhibit shows it happening on a tabletop. A running electrolysis cell moves copper through a low-cost ion exchange membrane and plates it out as solid metal while you watch, at room temperature, powered by less electricity than a laptop charger. The demonstration was built for a family crowd: the chemistry was picked because it produces no dangerous gases and no dangerous chemicals. Copper simply leaves one plate and grows on the other. Everything within reach of visitors is dry and safe to touch.

Around the live cell you can handle real results: vials of recovered metals and concentrates, membrane swatches you can touch, and a periodic table panel showing which elements this class of process can pull from waste streams. A short video loop shows the membrane being made from off-the-shelf materials in under two minutes.

The whole technology is open source. The build guide, the design files, and the patent license are public, so a motivated maker can leave this booth and build a working cell at home for less than the cost of a video game. Kids get to see metal appear out of a colored liquid. Builders leave with a project."
